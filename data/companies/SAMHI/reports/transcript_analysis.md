# Transcript Analysis — Samhi Hotels Ltd (SAMHI)

## Executive Summary
Across the last eight quarters, SAMHI Hotels has delivered a consistent same‑store growth trajectory anchored in strong office‑led demand, disciplined asset recycling, and progressive balance sheet repair. Management has steadily shifted the portfolio mix toward upscale/upper‑upscale assets while deleveraging from ~5x net debt/EBITDA post‑IPO to sub‑3x by FY26, aided by the GIC partnership. Guidance on RevPAR (9–11%), margins (~37–40% asset level), and capital allocation has largely been consistent and supported by delivery. The dominant themes are capital recycling, variable leases, and city‑centric business travel, with execution risk now concentrated in large projects like Navi Mumbai and W Hyderabad rather than core operations.

## Margin Commentary
Margins have been a recurring focus, with management consistently separating operating performance from accounting noise. Asset‑level EBITDA has trended from mid‑30s to ~40%, driven by rate‑led growth, ACIC integration, and lower finance costs. Temporary pressures such as GST changes (150–200 bps hit in Q3FY26) and renovation disruptions were clearly quantified, with credible levers (repricing, mix shift to upscale) outlined to offset them.

## Revenue Growth Narrative
Revenue growth has been predominantly organic and same‑store, driven by ADR expansion rather than occupancy, reflecting tight supply in Tier‑1 office markets. Inorganic growth has been selective and opportunistic (ACIC, Trinity Bangalore), followed by repositioning. Over time, the narrative has shifted from recovery and integration (FY24) to platform‑led compounding via upscale assets, variable leases, and large developments (FY26 onward), while remaining consistent on demand drivers.

## Key Risks Flagged by Management
- Event‑driven disruptions such as airline issues and geopolitical tensions impacting short‑term occupancy (Q3FY26, Q1FY26).
- GST regulation changes affecting reported margins temporarily (Q3FY26).
- Execution and timing risk on large developments like Navi Mumbai and W Hyderabad (Q2FY26).
- Dependence on office market absorption for sustained demand (Q2FY24 onward).

## Positive Signals
- ACIC portfolio margin improvement from ~30% to ~40% post integration (Q4FY25).
- Net debt/EBITDA reduced from ~5x post IPO to ~2.9x by Q2FY26.
- Strategic partnership with GIC providing capital and governance for upscale growth (AprFY25 Business Update).
- Consistent double‑digit same‑store RevPAR growth across multiple quarters (FY25–FY26).
- Rating upgrade to A+ with stable outlook (Q2FY26).

## Red Flags
- Large capex commitments in Navi Mumbai (~₹650+ cr) introduce execution and timing risk despite staggered spend.
- Occasional reliance on impairment reversals and deferred tax assets boosting reported PAT (Q4FY25, Q2FY26).
- GST margin impact initially framed as short‑term without precise offset timing (Q3FY26).

## Overall Investment Signal
**positive: Strong same‑store growth, improving margins, disciplined deleveraging, and consistent guidance delivery outweigh execution risks from large upcoming projects.**
