# Transcript Analysis — Yatharth Hospital and Trauma Care Services Ltd (YATHARTH)

## Executive Summary
Across the last eight quarters, Yatharth Hospitals has delivered strong revenue growth (consistently ~30%+ YoY, accelerating to ~45% in FY26) driven by aggressive capacity addition, super-specialty mix improvement and ARPOB expansion. Management has demonstrated execution capability in rapidly operationalising new hospitals (Greater Faridabad, Model Town Delhi, Sector-20 Faridabad) and integrating acquisitions, though near-term margins have seen dilution due to ramp-up losses. Guidance on margins, ARPOB growth and bed expansion has largely been consistent and conservative, with gradual payer-mix improvement away from government schemes. Overall, management quality appears robust, with increasing transparency, operational detail, and delivery broadly tracking stated plans, albeit with persistent working-capital and receivable challenges.

## Management Tone — IMPROVING
- Management consistently emphasized conservative margin guidance while explaining ramp-up losses transparently, e.g., 'Adjusting for Faridabad losses, our EBITDA margin would have been in the range of 26–27%' (Q3FY25).
- Clear acknowledgment and resolution path for income tax issues, culminating in asset unfreezing: 'order from income tax authorities unfreezing the provisional attached properties' (Q2FY26).
- Increasing specificity on ARPOB, occupancy and hospital-wise metrics over time, e.g., detailed ARPOB and occupancy disclosure by hospital in Q3FY26.
- Repeated articulation of realistic breakeven timelines: '15 to 18 months to reach EBITDA breakeven for new hospitals' (Q2FY26).

## Guidance vs Delivery

**Promised:** EBITDA margins to remain sustainable ~26–27% despite expansion; Faridabad acquisition to ramp quickly
**Delivered:** FY25 margins dipped to ~25% due to Faridabad losses but normalized to guided levels excluding new units

### Q2FY25 — EXCEEDED
**Promised:** Greater Faridabad to contribute meaningfully and move towards breakeven within 12–15 months
**Delivered:** Greater Faridabad reached EBITDA breakeven by Q1FY26

## Guidance Evolution Across Quarters
*Management sets directional targets and revises them transparently upward as execution improves, rather than quietly downgrading guidance.*

### EBITDA margin sustainability — Gradually Revised
- **Q3FY24:** Margins around 26–27% are sustainable even with new hospitals
- **Q3FY25:** Margins diluted temporarily due to Faridabad ramp-up; core margins intact
- **Q3FY26:** Adjusted EBITDA margin at ~29%, consolidated margins to stabilize at 24–25%

*Assessment: Management acknowledged short-term dilution but consistently explained it as temporary, with core margins intact.*

### Bed capacity expansion — Gradually Revised
- **Q2FY24:** Target to double bed capacity over ~3 years
- **Q3FY25:** Capacity addition via Delhi, Faridabad and brownfield expansions
- **Q3FY26:** Target revised upwards to ~5,000 beds over next 3–4 years

*Assessment: Expansion targets have been revised upward as execution confidence improved.*

### Government payer mix reduction — Consistent
- **Q3FY24:** Government mix ~35–40%, acceptable given capacity availability
- **Q3FY25:** Target to reduce government mix to ~25% over 2.5 years
- **Q3FY26:** New hospitals operating with <20% government mix from day one

*Assessment: Clear, well-articulated shift in payer mix with tangible progress.*

## Margin Commentary
Margins have been discussed candidly as a function of expansion phase. Core hospitals operate at ~28–30% EBITDA margins, while consolidated margins dip to ~24–25% during periods of heavy new-hospital ramp-up. Management consistently attributes margin pressure to temporary losses in new units and higher doctor costs, with confidence that occupancy ramp-up, ARPOB growth and CGHS rate revisions will offset this over time. Credibility is supported by adjusted margin disclosures and delivery on breakeven timelines.

## Revenue Growth Narrative
Revenue growth has been a mix of organic (occupancy ramp-up, ARPOB expansion, oncology and transplant programs) and inorganic (Greater Faridabad, Model Town Delhi, Sector-20 Faridabad, Agra). The narrative has remained consistent: expand within NCR/North India, focus on super-specialties, leverage medical tourism and reduce low-yield government business. Growth drivers have not shifted abruptly, indicating strategic coherence.

## Key Risks Flagged by Management
- Ramp-up losses and initial EBITDA drag from new hospitals (multiple quarters including Q3FY25, Q2FY26).
- Working capital and delayed government receivables impacting cash flows (Q3FY24, Q2FY25).
- Doctor and nursing attrition, particularly nursing staff in North India (Q4FY25, Q3FY26).
- Timing risk in empanelment for new hospitals affecting early occupancy (Q2FY25, Q2FY26).

## Positive Signals
- Greater Faridabad reaching EBITDA breakeven within ~12 months (Q3FY26).
- New Delhi and Sector-20 Faridabad achieving ARPOB above group average in initial quarters (Q3FY26).
- Consistent ARPOB growth of ~8–10% YoY across mature hospitals (Q3FY26).
- Resolution progress on income tax matter with unfreezing of assets (Q2FY26).
- Strong medical tourism traction with overseas OPDs and partnerships (Q3FY26).

## Red Flags
- Persistently high receivable days driven by government business despite repeated mitigation efforts.
- Heavy reliance on star doctors could increase fixed cost rigidity if volumes disappoint.
- Aggressive expansion pace increases execution risk, especially simultaneous ramp-up of multiple hospitals.

## Overall Investment Signal
**positive: Strong execution on growth and expansion, improving ARPOB and payer mix, and credible management outweigh near-term margin dilution and working-capital risks.**
