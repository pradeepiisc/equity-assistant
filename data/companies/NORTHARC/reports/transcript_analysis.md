# Transcript Analysis — Northern Arc Capital Ltd (NORTHARC)

## Executive Summary
Across the last six quarters, Northern Arc Capital has demonstrated a steady recovery and strengthening trajectory after FY25 stress, with AUM growth re-accelerating to 20%+ run-rate, improving ROA (2.4% → 2.7%), and stabilising credit costs around 2.6–3.0%. Management has been consistent in prioritising risk-adjusted returns over headline growth, evidenced by calibrated MFI exposure, conservative provisioning, and disciplined MSME expansion. Fee-based and technology-led credit solutions remain a structural differentiator, though monetisation is still early-stage. Overall, execution credibility has improved as guidance on growth, margins, and credit costs has largely aligned with delivery by Q3FY26.

## Management Tone — IMPROVING
- Q1FY26: Management acknowledged FY25 headwinds and stated that 'residual impact of FY25 stress will linger through the first half of the year' but set clear recovery expectations for H2.
- Q2FY26: Management explicitly reiterated credit cost guidance of 2.6–2.8% and AUM growth of 20–22%, signalling confidence without over-promising.
- Q3FY26: CEO stated 'incremental PAR 0+ accretion has reverted to pre-stress level' and backed it with vintage and guardrail data, reflecting higher specificity.
- Q4FY25: Management openly discussed one-time DLG provisioning and management overlay, clearly separating accounting prudence from underlying business performance.

## Guidance vs Delivery
### Q1FY26 — DELIVERED
**Promised:** H2FY26 credit cost to moderate to 2.7–2.9% and AUM growth to re-accelerate
**Delivered:** By Q2FY26, credit cost improved to 2.7% and AUM growth reached 15% YoY with momentum picking up

### Q2FY26 — ON_TRACK
**Promised:** Full-year FY26 credit cost of ~2.6–2.8% and ROA ~2.8%
**Delivered:** Q3FY26 exit credit cost guided at 2.7–3.0% and ROA improved to 2.7%

## Guidance Evolution Across Quarters
*Management guidance has been realistic, conservative, and largely unchanged over time, with delivery improving visibility and credibility.*

### Credit cost normalisation — Consistent
- **Q2FY25:** Credit costs elevated due to MFI stress; provisioning increased prudently.
- **Q4FY25:** Ex-DLG credit cost would have been ~2.6%.
- **Q1FY26:** H2 credit cost expected at 2.7–2.9%.
- **Q3FY26:** Exit credit cost expected to remain within 2.7–3.0%.

*Assessment: Management steadily guided and delivered a normalisation path without sharp reversals.*

### D2C mix expansion — Consistent
- **Q2FY25:** Target to increase D2C mix to ~70% by FY28.
- **Q1FY26:** D2C at 53%; strategy unchanged.
- **Q3FY26:** D2C reached 56%, growing ~30% YoY.

*Assessment: Clear execution against a multi-year stated objective.*

## Margin Commentary
Margins have been discussed primarily through a risk-adjusted lens rather than headline NIM. Management consistently cited mix shift towards D2C, declining cost of funds, and fee income as key levers. NIM improved from ~9.1% in FY25 to ~9.9% in Q3FY26, supported by ~90 bps YoY reduction in cost of funds. Margin guidance has remained credible, with no abrupt reversals.

## Revenue Growth Narrative
Revenue growth has been positioned as a combination of balance-sheet-led lending (MSME, consumer) and capital-light fee income (placements, funds, tech platforms). While FY25 growth was muted by MFI stress, FY26 saw revival led by MSME (+40%+ YoY) and consumer finance. The narrative around growth drivers has remained consistent, with no sudden pivots.

## Key Risks Flagged by Management
- MFI over-leveraging and borrower stress (Q2FY25, Q4FY25)
- Stress in unsecured small-ticket MSME loans (Q3FY26)
- Lag in transmission of repo rate cuts to MCLR affecting liquidity (Q1FY26, Q3FY26)
- Muted offshore funding participation (Q3FY26)

## Positive Signals
- AUM crossed ₹15,000 crore milestone (Q3FY26)
- Highest-ever quarterly PAT of ₹101 crore (Q3FY26)
- PAR 0+ accretion in MFI turned negative sequentially (Q3FY26)
- Cost of funds declined ~90 bps YoY to 8.5% (Q3FY26)
- Fee income grew ~50% YoY driven by placements (Q3FY26)

## Red Flags
- Repeated need for management overlays and one-time provisions in FY25 raised questions on initial risk calibration.
- Intermediate retail credit cost volatility attributed to 'prudence' rather than specific asset issues, limiting transparency.

## Overall Investment Signal
**positive: Improving execution, stabilised asset quality, and credible delivery against guidance outweigh residual sectoral risks.**
