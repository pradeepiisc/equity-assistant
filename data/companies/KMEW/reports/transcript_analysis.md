# Transcript Analysis — Knowledge Marine & Engineering Works Ltd (KMEW)

## Executive Summary
Across the last eight quarters, Knowledge Marine & Engineering Works (KMEW) has demonstrated a sharp scale-up in revenues, order book and asset base, driven by dredging, inland waterways, and international expansion (Myanmar, Bahrain). Management has consistently emphasized high-margin discipline (30–40% EBITDA) and long-tenure contracts, while progressively moving up the contract size ladder. The tone has shifted from aspirational (FY22–FY23) to more confident and execution-backed (FY24–FY26), supported by visible order inflows and asset deployment. However, repeated reliance on government decision timelines and periodic revenue deferrals remain execution risks. Overall, management credibility has improved with delivery broadly tracking guidance, especially from FY24 onwards.

## Management Tone — IMPROVING
- Management reiterated a clear margin floor: "We only bid for contracts which give us at least 30% EBITDA margins" (H1 FY23, Nov 2022).
- In FY24–FY25 calls, management provided quantified revenue and profit guidance, e.g., "We are planning for INR270–300 crores topline in FY25" (FY24 call, May 2024).
- Greater candour on delays: management explicitly attributed FY24 revenue dip to Myanmar unrest and delayed recognition (FY24 call, May 2024).
- In Q3 FY26, management confidently stated: "This quarter marks one of the strongest quarters in our recent history" backed by YoY and QoQ numbers (Q3 FY26, Feb 2026).
- Specific balance sheet and capital allocation disclosures (debt ₹166 cr, CWIP ₹36 cr, fund-use split) in Q3 FY26 indicate higher transparency versus earlier years.

## Guidance vs Delivery

## Guidance Evolution Across Quarters
*Management tends to set ambitious targets initially, then recalibrates based on execution realities. Revisions are openly discussed rather than quietly dropped, suggesting improving realism.*

### EBITDA margin target — Gradually Revised
- **FY22:** Target EBITDA margin of at least 40%
- **H1FY23:** Minimum sustainable margin of 30%
- **FY24:** Margins to remain around 30%
- **Q3FY26:** Current results can be considered benchmark

*Assessment: Management moderated margin expectations post-FY22 but has largely delivered within the revised 30–40% band.*

### Inland Waterways (IWAI) scale-up — Consistent
- **FY23:** Exploring IWAI river dredging opportunities
- **FY24:** Secured first IWAI contracts worth ~₹172 cr
- **Q3FY26:** IWAI order book ~₹230 cr, execution underway

*Assessment: Clear progression from bidding to execution with asset deployment.*

### International expansion (Bahrain sand mining) — Gradually Revised
- **FY24:** Entered Bahrain with ₹450 cr order book
- **FY25:** Initial ramp-up phase, revenue ₹15 cr booked
- **Q3FY26:** Actively scouting additional vessel for Bahrain

*Assessment: Execution slower initially, but management has been transparent and continues to pursue expansion.*

## Margin Commentary
Across quarters, management has consistently emphasized a margin floor of 30% EBITDA, attributing variability to dry-docking cycles, monsoon downtime, and subcontracting. Exceptional margins in FY22–FY23 were acknowledged as driven by unique Myanmar contracts, while later guidance was moderated. Credibility is supported by Q3FY26 margins rebounding to 43%, indicating strong operating leverage when utilization is high.

## Revenue Growth Narrative
Revenue growth has been largely organic, driven by larger contract eligibility, asset additions, and geographic expansion. Management consistently cites inland waterways, port expansion, and international dredging/sand mining as drivers. While FY24 saw a dip due to delayed Myanmar recognition, the broader narrative of scale-up has remained intact and resumed strongly in FY25–FY26.

## Key Risks Flagged by Management
- Delay in government tender decision-making and approvals (multiple quarters FY23–FY25).
- Operational disruptions due to monsoon and dry-docking cycles (H1FY23, FY24).
- Geopolitical and local unrest affecting international projects, e.g., Myanmar (FY24).

## Positive Signals
- Order book expansion from ~₹200 cr (FY23) to ~₹1,500 cr (Q3FY26).
- Entry into Bahrain with long-tenure ₹450 cr sand mining contracts (FY24).
- Strong Q3FY26 profitability with PAT margin 34% (Q3FY26).
- Successful scale-up into shipbuilding and green tug segments with 15-year contracts (FY26).

## Red Flags
- Repeated reliance on 'government delays' for missed revenue recognition (FY23–FY24).
- Earlier aggressive margin guidance (40%+) later toned down to 30%, indicating initial optimism bias.

## Overall Investment Signal
**positive: Strong order book visibility, improving management credibility, and sustained high margins outweigh execution and timing risks inherent in government-linked dredging businesses.**
