# Transcript Analysis — Ujjivan Small Finance Bank Ltd (UJJIVANSFB)

## Executive Summary
Across Q1–Q3 FY26, Ujjivan Small Finance Bank has demonstrated a clear recovery from microfinance stress, with steady improvement in collections, moderation in slippages, and improving profitability. Management has consistently executed on a strategy of shifting the loan mix toward secured products, lowering cost of funds, and stabilizing asset quality. Guidance on credit cost normalization, NIM stabilization, and ROA improvement has largely held quarter after quarter, indicating reasonable forecasting discipline. While some pockets of stress (notably Karnataka, West Bengal, parts of IL) persist, the overall trajectory is one of gradual normalization with improving operating leverage.

## Management Tone — IMPROVING
- Q1FY26: Management acknowledged stress clearly – 'Karnataka peaking in Q1… slippages are expected to show an improving trend going forward.'
- Q2FY26: Tone became more confident – 'slippages have started to reduce… SMA book as of September ’25 is below 2%, the lowest since Q1 ’25.'
- Q3FY26: Strong conviction – 'broad-based improvements delivered in quarter 3 across key operating metrics are durable and reflective of disciplined execution.'
- Q3FY26: Clear forward visibility – 'we expect NIM to at least stay at the same level… possibilities for improvement do exist.'

## Guidance vs Delivery
### Q1FY26 — DELIVERED
**Promised:** Credit cost to improve in H2; ROA 1.2–1.4%, ROE 10–12% for FY26
**Delivered:** Q2 commentary showed slippages reduced from ~₹350 cr to ₹278 cr and credit cost guided to decline in H2

### Q2FY26 — EXCEEDED
**Promised:** Meaningful decline in credit cost in H2; NIM to stabilize ~7.8–7.9%
**Delivered:** Q3FY26 NIM reported at 8.2%; credit cost moderated with lower slippages and higher PCR

### Q3FY26 — PENDING
**Promised:** FY26 ROA 1.2–1.4%, ROE 10–12%; further credit cost moderation in Q4
**Delivered:** Pending – Q4FY26 results awaited

## Guidance Evolution Across Quarters
*Management guidance shows continuity rather than optimism bias. Targets are reiterated with incremental evidence, suggesting realism and internal alignment.*

### Credit cost normalization — Consistent
- **Q1FY26:** Credit cost elevated in H1; improvement expected in H2.
- **Q2FY26:** Meaningful decline in credit cost anticipated in second half.
- **Q3FY26:** Normalization expected by end of Q1–Q2 FY27; Q4 FY26 lower than Q3.

*Assessment: Management has stuck to the same normalization narrative and early data supports this trajectory.*

### Secured book mix increase — Consistent
- **Q1FY26:** Secured portfolio share increased from 31% to 46% YoY.
- **Q2FY26:** Secured loans now 47%; on track for 35%+ growth in FY26.
- **Q3FY26:** Secured share reached 48%; long-term target 65–70% by FY30.

*Assessment: Execution aligns well with long-term strategic intent.*

## Margin Commentary
Margins were initially under pressure in Q1FY26 due to excess liquidity, repricing impacts, and one-offs, but management consistently guided for stabilization. By Q2FY26, NIM improved to 7.9% and further expanded to 8.2% in Q3FY26, driven by lower cost of funds, better product mix, and normalization of interest reversals. Commentary around margins has been specific and credible, supported by concrete levers like SA/TD repricing and CRR relief.

## Revenue Growth Narrative
Revenue growth has been driven primarily by organic expansion across secured products (affordable housing, MSME, gold, vehicle, micro-mortgages) and recovery in microfinance disbursements. Management has consistently emphasized calibrated growth rather than aggressive expansion, with no inorganic growth narrative. The growth story has remained stable across quarters, anchored in diversification and cross-sell.

## Key Risks Flagged by Management
- Microfinance stress in specific states like Karnataka, Gujarat, West Bengal (Q1FY26, Q2FY26).
- Competitive intensity and yield pressure in Affordable Housing (Q3FY26).
- Election-related disruptions in certain states (West Bengal, Tamil Nadu) (Q3FY26).
- Regulatory uncertainty around Universal Bank license timing (Q1–Q3FY26).

## Positive Signals
- Bucket X collection efficiency improving to 99.7% by December 2025 (Q3FY26).
- Highest-ever quarterly disbursements of ₹8,293 cr in Q3FY26.
- Cost of funds reduced to 7.08%, down 26 bps QoQ (Q3FY26).
- PCR improved to 76% with expectations of further improvement (Q3FY26).
- Secured portfolio growth >50% YoY sustained across quarters (Q2–Q3FY26).

## Red Flags
- Repeated deferral on clarity around Universal Bank license – 'we have to just wait for their decision' (Q3FY26).
- IL PAR remained sticky in select states despite overall improvement (Q2FY26, Q3FY26).
- Some reliance on future quarters for full credit cost normalization (Q1–Q3FY26).

## Overall Investment Signal
**positive: Asset quality and profitability are on a clear recovery path, guidance has been consistent and largely delivered, and strategic shift toward secured lending is reducing volatility, though some execution and regulatory risks remain.**
