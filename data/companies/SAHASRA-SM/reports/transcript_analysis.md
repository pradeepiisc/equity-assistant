# Transcript Analysis — Sahasra Electronics Solutions Ltd (SAHASRA-SM)

## Executive Summary
Across the three quarters, Sahasra Electronics shows a clear transition from over-optimistic, export-heavy guidance (H1FY25) to a more grounded, execution-focused narrative by H1FY26. FY25 saw revenue and margin disappointment driven by export slowdown, inventory overhang, and delayed semiconductor ramp-up, leading to credibility pressure from investors. By H1FY26, management delivered visible recovery in EMS, better cost control, healthier order book, and clearer disclosure on semiconductor delays and realities. Key themes are diversification away from US exports, gradual stabilization of EMS as cash engine, and patient, subsidy-supported semiconductor scaling.

## Guidance Evolution Across Quarters
*Early guidance was aspirational and aggressive; over time, management recalibrated expectations, acknowledged delays, and anchored projections to confirmed orders and subsidies.*

### Revenue growth targets — Gradually Revised
- **H1FY25:** We are looking to end this year with a 20% growth or more.

- **H1FY26:** We have achieved ₹58.16 Cr in H1 and are on track for ₹130 Cr EMS revenue.

*Assessment: Targets were scaled down and made more execution-linked after misses in FY25.*

### Semiconductor ramp-up — Deferred Repeatedly

- **H1FY26:** eSIM approvals delayed; recovery expected from Mar–Apr onwards.

*Assessment: Semiconductor scale-up timelines have slipped multiple times, though reasons are now clearly articulated.*

## Margin Commentary
Margins were initially presented as structurally strong (~20% PAT, ~30% EBITDA in H1FY25), but FY25 saw contraction due to inventory build-up, higher R&D costs, and export softness. By H2FY25, management acknowledged margins dropping to ~9% in H2. In H1FY26, EBITDA stabilized at ~12.6% and PAT ~15.5%, with management focusing more on cost control, mix improvement, and volume-led operating leverage rather than lofty margin claims, improving credibility.

## Revenue Growth Narrative
The revenue story shifted from export-led hypergrowth (US-focused EMS and Thomson IT hardware) to a more balanced mix. FY25 pivoted toward domestic EMS (EV chargers, GPS, metering) to offset export weakness. By H1FY26, exports were ~50–55% vs ~85–90% earlier, reflecting diversification. Semiconductor revenue remains a medium-term option value rather than a near-term driver, a shift from earlier narratives.

## Key Risks Flagged by Management
- US tariff uncertainty impacting export order visibility (H1FY25, H2FY25)
- Customer inventory overhang delaying order placements (H1FY25, H2FY25)
- Long approval and qualification cycles in semiconductor business (H1FY25, H1FY26)
- Dependence on government subsidy timing for semiconductor economics (H2FY25, H1FY26)

## Positive Signals
- Order book at ~40% of FY26 target early in the year (H2FY25)
- EMS revenue recovery and expense control in H1FY26 (H1FY26)
- eSIM contract signed with European customer after audit clearance (H1FY26)
- SPEC subsidy process completed at IFCI level, disbursement expected (H1FY26)
- Initiation of merger process with unlisted EMS, PCB, and skilling entities (H1FY26)

## Red Flags
- Repeatedly optimistic semiconductor timelines slipping quarter after quarter (H1FY25 → H1FY26)
- Investor complaints about guidance credibility and execution gaps (H2FY25)
- Confusion around financial disclosures (cash flow write-off query) requiring clarification (H1FY26)

## Overall Investment Signal
**cautious: Core EMS business shows credible recovery and better execution discipline, but semiconductor upside remains back-ended with repeated timeline slippages. Valuation comfort and patience are key.**
