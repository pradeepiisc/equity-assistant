# Transcript Analysis — Newjaisa Technologies Ltd (NEWJAISA-SM)

## Executive Summary
Across the last six quarters, Newjaisa’s narrative shifts from confident, high-growth execution post-IPO (FY24) to a sharp operational and financial disruption in FY25 driven by Amazon’s exit as a refurbished marketplace. Management responded with channel diversification (D2C, B2B, education, offline retail), cost rationalisation, and heavy marketing spend, achieving partial revenue recovery but at the cost of margins and cash burn. The trajectory shows resilience and speed of response, but also exposes concentration risk, inventory management challenges, and dependence on execution of new channels. Overall, management quality shows adaptability but with rising complexity and execution risk.

## Margin Commentary
Margins were initially discussed as improving through operational efficiency (repair vs replace) in FY24. By FY25, margins came under pressure from higher employee costs, inventory markdowns, low-margin trading transactions, and channel mix shift toward desktops and B2B. In H2 FY25 and H1 FY26, management openly acknowledged negative EBITDA and guided for continued margin pressure due to marketing spend and low capacity utilisation, lending credibility but highlighting structural fragility.

## Revenue Growth Narrative
The growth story transitioned from pure demand-led, online-first expansion (FY24) to a forced reorientation after Amazon’s exit. Management now positions growth as multi-channel: D2C website, enterprise/SME, education, and offline retail. Growth since Feb FY25 has been recovery-led rather than expansionary, with partial substitution of lost marketplace volumes rather than net new demand.

## Key Risks Flagged by Management
- Dependence on a single marketplace partner (Amazon) – highlighted in H2 FY25.
- Inventory aging and markdown risk – discussed in H2 FY25 and H1 FY26.
- High fixed-cost structure and low capacity utilisation – H2 FY25, H1 FY26.
- Competitive pressure from small sellers and platforms – H1 FY25.
- Working capital intensity due to inventory-led model – H1 FY24, H1 FY25.

## Positive Signals
- Recovery of ~65–75% of lost Amazon revenue via new channels (H1 FY26).
- Strong growth in own website traffic and sales (100%+ post-Feb FY25).
- Addition of enterprise clients including large NBFCs and EMS players (H2 FY25, H1 FY26).
- R2 and e-waste related certifications enabling direct corporate sourcing (H2 FY25, H1 FY26).
- Cost rationalisation with ~40% workforce reduction to manage burn (H1 FY26).
