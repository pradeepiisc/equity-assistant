# Transcript Analysis — Som Distilleries and Breweries Ltd (SDBL)

## Executive Summary
Across the eight quarters analysed (Q1FY23 to Q2FY26), Som Distilleries delivered strong multi‑year revenue and volume growth driven by beer-led expansion, capacity additions, and market share gains in MP, Karnataka, Odisha and newer states. Management consistently highlighted operational execution, distribution strength, and portfolio breadth as key levers, while margins oscillated within a 12–15% EBITDA band amid raw material volatility and regulatory shocks. However, guidance quality weakened in FY25–FY26, with repeated regulatory disruptions (notably Karnataka excise) and visible slippage between volume/revenue guidance and delivery. Overall, execution capability is evident, but guidance credibility and regulatory exposure have become more pronounced risks.

## Margin Commentary
Across quarters, management consistently cited raw material inflation (barley, glass, sugar) and packaging mix as key margin drivers. FY23–FY24 saw margin compression due to heavy use of new glass bottles amid rapid growth, with partial offsets from price hikes and mix improvement. From FY25 onward, margins benefited from stabilized raw material prices, higher returnable glass usage, and can mix, enabling occasional upside to 14–15%. Margin guidance has been cautious and broadly credible, though sensitive to regulatory and volume swings.

## Revenue Growth Narrative
Revenue growth has been predominantly organic, driven by beer volumes, brand scaling (Hunter, Power Cool, Black Fort), and geographic expansion. Management consistently emphasized distribution reach and value-for-money positioning rather than aggressive advertising. IMFL remained a secondary contributor (~10–11%), with recent attempts at premiumization (Mahavat, single malt plans). The growth narrative has remained largely consistent, though FY26 saw disruption-led slowdown versus earlier hyper-growth.

## Key Risks Flagged by Management
- Regulatory and excise duty volatility, especially in Karnataka (Q4FY25, Q2FY26)
- Raw material price inflation and dependence on glass bottles (Q1FY23, Q4FY23, Q4FY24)
- Working capital strain due to duty-paid state structures (Q1FY23, Q4FY24)
- Seasonality and monsoon impact on volumes (multiple quarters)

## Positive Signals
- Consistent market share gains in MP, Karnataka and Odisha (Q1FY23, Q2FY24)
- Successful capacity expansions at Hassan and Odisha plants (Q4FY23, Q4FY25)
- Strong debt reduction and credit rating upgrade to A- (Q2FY25)
- Entry into new markets like Rajasthan, Jharkhand, Tamil Nadu (Q4FY24, Q2FY26)

## Red Flags
- Repeated refusal to provide granular guidance or data, citing prudence (multiple quarters)
- Visible tension and defensive tone in Q4FY25 regarding funding and equity questions
- Guidance slippage in FY26 due to regulatory shocks after optimistic growth commentary
